# Components of Intelligence (Perception, Reasoning, Learning)

Intelligence, whether human or artificial, involves three primary computational abilities that allow an entity to interact successfully with the world.

## 1. Interaction with the Real World (Perception and Action)

This involves the agent's ability to sense and affect its environment.

- **Perception:** Recognizing and understanding inputs (e.g., speech recognition, image understanding).
    
- **Action:** Ability to take actions and have a physical effect in the world (e.g., robotics, controlling a vehicle).
    

## 2. Reasoning and Planning

This is the internal computation required to solve problems and make decisions.

- **Modeling the World:** Creating an internal representation of the external world based on perceived input.
    
- **Problem Solving:** Solving new problems, creating long-term plans, and making immediate decisions.
    
- **Uncertainty:** The ability to deal with unexpected problems and uncertainties inherent in the real world.
    

## 3. Learning and Adaptation

This involves improving the agent's capabilities over time based on experience.

- **Continuous Update:** Internal models of the world are constantly updated.
    
- **Example:** A baby learning to categorize and recognize objects or animals.
    
- **Key Idea:** Machine learning allows computers to perform tasks without being explicitly programmed for every scenario.
