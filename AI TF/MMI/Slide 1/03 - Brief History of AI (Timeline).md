# Brief History of AI (Timeline)

Understanding the history helps put current advancements in context and shows how the field adapts when major limitations are found.

|Period|Key Event / Theme|Important Figures/Systems|
|---|---|---|
|**1943**|**Early Beginnings**|**McCulloch & Pitts** proposed the Boolean circuit model of the brain (early neural networks).|
|**1950**|**Turing**|**Alan Turing** published "Computing Machinery and Intelligence," proposing the Turing Test.|
|**1956**|**The Birth of AI**|**Dartmouth Meeting**, where the term "**Artificial Intelligence**" was formally adopted.|
|**1950s - 1965**|**Initial Promise & Great Enthusiasm**|**Samuel's** checkers program, **Newell & Simon's** Logic Theorist and General Problem Solver (GPS), and **McCarthy's** invention of LISP.|
|**1966 - 1973**|**Reality Dawns**|Realization that many AI problems were **intractable**. Limitations of early neural network methods were identified, leading to a temporary decline in that research area.|
|**1969 - 1985**|**Knowledge-Based Systems**|Focus shifted to adding domain-specific knowledge. Development of successful rule-based **Expert Systems** (e.g., DENDRAL, MYCIN). However, these systems were often **brittle** and did not scale well.|
|**1986 -**|**Rise of Machine Learning**|Neural networks regained popularity. Major advances in Machine Learning algorithms and applications began.|
|**1990 -**|**The Role of Uncertainty**|Adoption of **Bayesian networks** as a robust framework for representing knowledge and dealing with uncertainty.|
|**1995 -**|**AI as a Science**|Integration of learning, reasoning, and knowledge representation. AI methods became integral to fields like computer vision, natural language processing, and data mining.|
