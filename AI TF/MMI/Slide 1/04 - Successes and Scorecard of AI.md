# Successes and Scorecard of AI

AI has evolved from a theoretical pursuit to an applied science, yielding impressive and impactful results.

## Major Successes

- **Chess Champion:** In 1997, **Deep Blue** defeated the reigning world chess champion, Garry Kasparov, showcasing complex search and calculation capabilities.
    
- **Autonomous Driving:** The **DARPA Grand Challenge** (2003-2007) stimulated massive research. In 2005, the **Stanley** robot (Stanford Racing Team) won the 132-mile race, finishing completely unassisted.
    
- **Mathematical Proof:** An AI program proved a long-unsolved mathematical conjecture (**Robbins Conjecture**).
    
- **Logistics:** During the 1991 Gulf War, US forces used an AI logistics planning and scheduling program involving up to 50,000 entities.
    
- **NASA Planning:** NASA's autonomous planning program controlled scheduling operations for a spacecraft.
    
- **Crossword Puzzles:** The Proverb program solves crossword puzzles better than most humans.
    
- **Consumer Technology:** Face recognition software became available in consumer cameras around 2006.
    

## The AI Scorecard (Can Machines...?)

|Task|Conclusion|Notes|
|---|---|---|
|**Beat Humans at Chess?**|**YES**|Today's computers can beat the best human players (e.g., Deep Blue).|
|**Talk (Speech Synthesis)?**|**YES** (for words/short phrases), **NO** (for complete, natural sentences)|Systems handle text-to-phonetic conversion well, but struggle with natural emphasis and emotion since they lack true understanding.|
|**Recognize Speech?**|**YES** (for restricted vocabulary/single speaker), **NO** (for normal, continuous speech)|Highly accurate for constrained tasks (like directory inquiries). Normal, continuous speech has word boundary issues, large vocabularies, and contextual ambiguity.|
|**Understand Speech?**|**NO**|Understanding meaning requires immense **implicit commonsense knowledge** (e.g., resolving ambiguity in "Time flies like an arrow").|
|**Learn and Adapt?**|**YES**|Machine learning allows computers to adapt without explicit programming (e.g., **RALPH** at CMU drove 98% of the way from Pittsburgh to San Diego unassisted).|
|**"See" (Computer Vision)?**|**YES** (for constrained problems), **NO** (for understanding real-world scenes)|Works for things like hand-written zip code recognition or face recognition. Understanding a full 3D map from a 2D image remains difficult.|
|**Plan and Make Optimal Decisions?**|**YES** (for constrained problems), **NO** (for general real-world problems)|Effective for well-defined problems (like chess). Real-world planning (like booking a holiday) is too complex due to uncertainty and the huge number of details (the need for **commonsense** to constrain solutions).|

_Many of the "NO" answers point to the core issues that make up the field's research problems, which rely on the integration of several knowledge areas: [[05 - Academic Disciplines Relevant to AI]]_
