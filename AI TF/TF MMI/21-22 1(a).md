# 1. (a) AI and The Programmer Problem

The question is whether computers are limited to only what programmers tell them, and whether this limits intelligence. The answer lies in the difference between **explicit instructions** and **emergent behavior**.

## 1. The Premise: "Computers only do what programmers tell them." (FALSE for AI)

This premise is true for simple, classic programs (like a `for` loop or a calculator). It is **false** for advanced AI systems like the **Genetic Algorithm (GA)**.

|Algorithm Type|Programmer's Role|System's Output|
|---|---|---|
|**Classical (e.g., A*)**|**Explicitly codes the path.**|Predictable; exactly what was coded.|
|**Evolutionary (GA)**|**Only codes the goal (Fitness Function).**|**Novel:** Generates solutions the programmer never saw or wrote.|

### The GA Counter-Argument

In the GA, the programmer gives the system the rules of **evolution**, not the solution itself:

1. **Rule 1: Goal:** "Here is a function $F(x)$. Find $x$ that maximizes $F(x)$."
    
2. **Rule 2: Variation:** "Use **random mutation** and **random crossover** to mix and change solutions."
    

The final high-scoring solution is built over thousands of generations by **random chance** and **selection pressure**. The specific, optimized structure is an **emergent property** that the programmer did not define in the source code.

## 2. The Implication: "Does this limit intelligence?" (FALSE)

Since the system generates **unforeseen, novel, and optimized solutions** on its own through autonomous search, the premise fails.

- **Intelligence** in AI is often defined by the ability to generate **novelty** and achieve high-level goals without step-by-step guidance.
    
- The GA achieves the goal **without a predefined path**. This ability demonstrates a level of independent problem-solving that contradicts the idea that the computer is merely a slave to its lines of code.
    

**Conclusion:** The ability of Evolutionary Algorithms to discover solutions through stochastic (random) means proves that the computer can move **beyond the explicit instructions of the programmer**, undermining the fundamental argument against machine intelligence.
