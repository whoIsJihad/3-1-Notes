
The **PEAS** description is a standard way in Artificial Intelligence to characterize the task environment for an intelligent agent. PEAS stands for:

- **P**erformance measure
    
- **E**nvironment
    
- **A**ctuators
    
- **S**ensors
    


---

## 🤖 PEAS Description for AI Task Environments

Here is the step-by-step PEAS breakdown for each activity:

### (i) Bidding on an item at an auction

The intelligent agent in this case is the **bidding agent** itself, programmed to maximize the user's return or secure the item for the lowest possible price.

|**Element**|**Description**|**Example/Details**|
|---|---|---|
|**P**erformance Measure|Maximizing profit (winning the item at a low price or securing it below a specific value) and/or minimizing loss.|_Example:_ The utility gained from the item minus the price paid. Success is often binary (win/lose), but the measure is continuous (profit margin).|
|**E**nvironment|The auction market, which includes the item being sold, the rules of the auction (e.g., English, Dutch, sealed-bid), and the other bidders.|**Characteristics:** Usually **Stochastic** (other bidders' actions are uncertain), **Episodic** (each auction is a single, self-contained event), **Dynamic** (the price/state changes while the agent is deliberating), **Discrete** (bids are specific, discrete amounts), and **Multi-Agent**.|
|**A**ctuators|The mechanisms the agent uses to affect the environment.|Placing a bid, canceling a bid, adjusting the maximum bid limit.|
|**S**ensors|The inputs the agent receives about the state of the auction.|Current highest bid, time remaining, the identity of the highest bidder (if available), the item description, and its estimated value.|

### (ii) Medical diagnosis system

The intelligent agent here is the **diagnosis system**, which processes patient data to determine a possible illness or condition.

|**Element**|**Description**|**Example/Details**|
|---|---|---|
|**P**erformance Measure|Accuracy of diagnosis, correct treatment recommendations, minimized patient suffering and cost, maximizing positive patient outcomes.|_Example:_ Percentage of correct diagnoses over time, speed of diagnosis, and minimizing malpractice risk.|
|**E**nvironment|The patient, the hospital/clinic environment, medical history databases, and the treating physician/user.|**Characteristics:** **Partially Observable** (not all internal patient states or symptoms are known), **Stochastic** (patient responses to treatment or disease progression are uncertain), **Sequential** (diagnosis is often an ongoing process with tests and follow-ups), **Dynamic** (patient state can change), and **Discrete** (the set of possible diagnoses is finite).|
|**A**ctuators|The mechanisms the agent uses to provide output.|Displaying the diagnosis, recommending further tests (e.g., MRI, blood test), suggesting a treatment plan or medication.|
|**S**ensors|The patient data inputs the system receives.|Patient symptoms (e.g., fever, pain), lab test results, vital signs, medical images (X-rays), and patient history.|

---

### (iii) Interactive English tutor

The intelligent agent is the **English tutor program**, designed to help a student improve their language skills.

|**Element**|**Description**|**Example/Details**|
|---|---|---|
|**P**erformance Measure|Student learning rate/progress, retention of material, student satisfaction, and accuracy of the tutor's feedback.|_Example:_ Improvement in test scores, time spent on the system, and successful completion of learning objectives.|
|**E**nvironment|The student (their current knowledge, motivation, and input), the curriculum/lesson material, and the user interface.|**Characteristics:** **Partially Observable** (the student's internal cognitive state and focus are not fully known), **Stochastic** (student responses are unpredictable), **Sequential** (learning is a long, multi-step process), **Dynamic** (the student's knowledge and skill level change as they interact), and **Multi-Agent** (the student is a thinking agent).|
|**A**ctuators|The actions the tutor takes to teach, test, and guide the student.|Presenting new material, generating practice exercises, providing feedback (e.g., "Correct," "Try again," or detailed grammatical explanation), asking questions, and adjusting the lesson difficulty.|
|**S**ensors|The student's inputs and performance data.|Text entered by the student, answers to quizzes, time taken to respond, and tracked progress through the curriculum.|
