# Classical Algorithms vs. Evolutionary Algorithms & The Genetic Algorithm

This document addresses the fundamental differences between classical AI algorithms and evolutionary algorithms, followed by a detailed description of the Genetic Algorithm (GA).

## 1. Comparison of Classical and Evolutionary Algorithms
Read an easier explanation here [[ Classical Vs Evolutionary Algorithm]]
Classical AI algorithms (like Search, Planning, and Logical Inference) and Evolutionary Algorithms (like Genetic Algorithms, Evolutionary Programming) approach problem-solving from fundamentally different perspectives.

|Feature|Classical Algorithms|Evolutionary Algorithms|
|---|---|---|
|**Paradigm**|**Explicit Knowledge / Logical Deduction.** The solution is found by systematic exploration or inference based on defined rules.|**Implicit Knowledge / Biological Inspiration.** The solution emerges through iterative adaptation and selection, mimicking natural evolution.|
|**Search Space**|Generally **systematic and structured.** Focuses on state-space search (e.g., A*), satisfying constraints (e.g., CSP), or logical inference.|Generally **stochastic and global.** Focuses on optimization in large, complex, or ill-defined search spaces.|
|**Optimality**|Can often **guarantee optimality or completeness** (e.g., A* with admissible heuristic, complete theorem provers).|Typically finds a **near-optimal (sub-optimal) solution** quickly. Optimality is not guaranteed, but high-quality solutions are highly probable.|
|**Problem Type**|Best for problems with **clear state spaces, defined goals, and precise cost functions** (e.g., Maze Solving, Planning, Game Playing).|Best for **hard optimization problems** where the search space is vast, non-differentiable, or rugged (e.g., Scheduling, Function Optimization, Machine Learning).|
|**Knowledge Use**|Requires **domain-specific knowledge** to be encoded into the heuristic or rules.|Only requires a **fitness function** (objective function) to evaluate the quality of a potential solution.|
|**Robustness**|Can be brittle; a small change in rules or constraints may require a complete redesign of the search strategy.|**Highly robust** to changes in the problem landscape; can adapt to new constraints and handle noisy data easily.|

## 2. The Genetic Algorithm (GA) in Detail

The Genetic Algorithm (GA) is an evolutionary computation technique used to find optimal or near-optimal solutions to hard optimization and search problems. It is based on the principles of natural selection and genetics.

### 2.1. Core Concepts

- **Chromosome (Individual/Candidate Solution):** A representation of a single potential solution to the problem. It is usually encoded as a string of values (binary, integer, or real numbers).
    
- **Population:** A collection of many candidate solutions (chromosomes) that evolve over generations.
    
- **Fitness Function:** A function that takes a chromosome as input and returns a numerical value (fitness score) representing the quality of that solution. This is the **objective function** the algorithm tries to maximize.
    

### 2.2. The Five-Step Iterative Process

The GA starts with a randomly generated initial population and then iteratively applies the following steps over many **Generations**:

#### Step 1: Initialization

- A starting **population** of $N$ chromosomes is created, typically by generating random genetic material (random binary strings, random parameter values, etc.).
    

#### Step 2: Evaluation (Selection Preparation)

- Each chromosome in the current population is evaluated using the **fitness function**.
    
- The fitness score determines the probability of that individual being selected for reproduction. Higher fitness means a higher chance of survival and passing on genetic material.
    

#### Step 3: Selection

- A subset of the current population (parents) is selected to create the next generation. Common selection methods include:
    
    - **Roulette Wheel Selection:** Individuals are chosen with probability proportional to their fitness (like a spin on a roulette wheel).
        
    - **Tournament Selection:** A small group of individuals competes, and the fittest one wins and is selected.
        
- The selection process favors individuals with high fitness, ensuring that successful traits are propagated.
    

#### Step 4: Reproduction (Crossover)

- The selected parents are paired up, and they **reproduce** to create offspring (children).
    
- **Crossover (or Recombination):** Genetic material is exchanged between the two parents to create two new offspring chromosomes.
    
    - **Example (Single-Point Crossover):**
        
        - Parent A: `101 | 1001`
            
        - Parent B: `010 | 0110`
            
        - Child 1: `101 0110`
            
        - Child 2: `010 1001`
            
- Crossover is crucial for combining good partial solutions found in different individuals.
    

#### Step 5: Mutation

- After crossover, the newly created offspring are subjected to **mutation** with a small probability (mutation rate, typically $< 1\%$).
    
- **Mutation:** Randomly alters a small part of a chromosome (e.g., flipping a bit in a binary string).
    
- Mutation is essential for **maintaining genetic diversity** in the population and preventing the algorithm from getting stuck in a local optimum. It allows the search to explore new areas of the search space.
    

The offspring then form the new population, and the entire process repeats until a stopping criterion is met (e.g., maximum number of generations reached, or a satisfactory fitness level achieved).

### 2.3. Genetic Algorithm Pseudocode

```
function GENETIC_ALGORITHM(Population_Size, Max_Generations):
    
    // 1. Initialization
    P = INITIALIZE_POPULATION(Population_Size) 
    
    for i from 1 to Max_Generations:
        
        // 2. Evaluation
        P = EVALUATE_FITNESS(P)
        
        New_P = empty list
        
        // Loop until the New Population is full
        while size of New_P < Population_Size:
            
            // 3. Selection
            Parent1 = SELECTION(P) 
            Parent2 = SELECTION(P)
            
            // 4. Reproduction (Crossover)
            Child1, Child2 = CROSSOVER(Parent1, Parent2) 
            
            // 5. Mutation
            Child1 = MUTATE(Child1)
            Child2 = MUTATE(Child2)
            
            // Add the new individuals to the next generation
            New_P.ADD(Child1)
            New_P.ADD(Child2)
        
        // Replace the old population with the new one
        P = New_P 
        
        // Optional: Track the best solution found so far
        Best_Solution = Find_Fittest(P)
        
    return Best_Solution
```
