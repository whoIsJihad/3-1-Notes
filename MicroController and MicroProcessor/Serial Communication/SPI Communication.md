	# SPI Communication

**Tags:** #microcontroller #atmega32 #spi #serial

The **Serial Peripheral Interface (SPI)** is a synchronous serial communication protocol that is widely used for short-distance communication between a microcontroller and peripheral devices like sensors, SD cards, and shift registers.

### Master-Slave Architecture

SPI operates in a master-slave configuration.

- **Master**: The main controller (usually your ATmega32) that initiates the communication, generates the clock signal, and selects which slave to talk to.
    
- **Slave**: The peripheral device that responds to the master.
    

### The Four Wires of SPI

SPI communication typically uses four wires:

- `MISO` (Master In, Slave Out): The data line for the Slave to send data to the Master.
    
- `MOSI` (Master Out, Slave In): The data line for the Master to send data to the Slave.
    
- `SCK` (Serial Clock): The clock signal generated by the Master to synchronize the data transfer.
    
- `SS` (Slave Select): The line used by the Master to select which slave device it wants to communicate with. The master pulls this line low to activate a specific slave.
    

### How it Works

SPI communication is essentially a data exchange driven by the master's clock.

1. The Master pulls the `SS` line of the desired slave low.
    
2. Both the Master and the Slave have an 8-bit shift register. The Master generates a series of clock pulses on the `SCK` line.
    
3. On each clock pulse, one bit is shifted out from the Master's register onto the `MOSI` line and into the Slave's register.
    
4. Simultaneously, one bit is shifted out from the Slave's register onto the `MISO` line and into the Master's register.
    

After 8 clock pulses, the contents of the two shift registers have been completely swapped. This makes SPI a **full-duplex** protocol.
